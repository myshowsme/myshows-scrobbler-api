Anime is the only category where the episode number is ambiguous on its own, so it has a
few more rules.

## Which identifiers to send

Besides the common keys, there are five anime-specific ones: `anidb`, `anilist`, `kitsu`,
`mal`, `shikimori`. If you know any of them, send it. For anime they are more reliable
than `tmdb` and `tvdb`, because they don't depend on how a catalog split the series into
seasons.

If you know both `tmdb` and `tvdb`, send **both**. On split-cour titles (Attack on
Titan, Solo Leveling) the season boundaries in these two catalogs differ, and a single ID
can't tell which part of the series is meant.

::note
---
icon: i-lucide-medal
---
**IMDb comes first.** When identifiers conflict, `imdb` wins. Always send it when you
know it.
::

## Absolute numbering

Anime releases often have no season at all: the file name carries an absolute number,
like `Frieren - 12 [1080p].mkv`. In that case, send `episode.number` without
`episode.season`. That's valid, the field is optional.

::caution
---
icon: i-lucide-triangle-alert
---
**Don't convert the absolute number into a season and episode pair on your side.**
The episode identifier will help with matching.
::

The reference client works exactly like this: the file name parser extracts the
absolute number and leaves the season empty
([`filename-parser.ts`](https://github.com/myshowsme/myshows-scrobbler/blob/main/src/utils/filename-parser.ts)),
and the converter simply leaves the field out of the payload
([`converter.ts`](https://github.com/myshowsme/myshows-scrobbler/blob/main/src/scrobblers/converter.ts)).

## Specials, OVA and ONA

The reference client doesn't tell them apart: an OVA is sent as an episode of the parent
series with the number the source provided. Such releases are usually listed in the
catalog as special episodes of the parent series anyway.
